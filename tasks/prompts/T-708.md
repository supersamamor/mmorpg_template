### Goal
Characters can own outfits and choose which show. An outfit changes only how a character looks, never what it can do.

### Requirements
1. **Never stats.** An outfit has no modifiers, effects, requirements, price or drop, and cannot have them: the schema has no place for them and `validate` refuses anything that tries. A test proves the stat sheet, damage and speed are the same with and without every outfit.
2. **Data.** Outfits are content (`outfit.*`, the vocabulary's way): name, icon key, the slots they cover (a set of their own, as data or vocabulary: decide and say why), a piece per slot by asset key, optionally per gender, optionally taking colours.
   Every cross-reference is validated. The template ships two placeholder outfits made by `themegen`, original and plain; forks add their own. The client gets display data only.
3. **Owning.** Outfits a character owns are saved with it (a set of ids, each at most once) in T-702's transaction. An id the content has lost is kept and ignored, like a look (T-705), and comes back with the content.
   They are granted by one generic effect or reward primitive usable from data (a quest reward at least), so T-709's store can grant them later on the server; nothing is sold here. The placeholder outfits are given by existing content in one small way: decide.
   Decide whether they belong to the character or the account, and say what that means for T-709 and for deleting a character.
4. **Choosing.** One intent sets what shows in a slot (or nothing). The server checks that it is owned, that it covers the slot and that the gender fits, and refuses with a term. It survives a save and a reconnect. Allowed in the world, also in a fight (it changes nothing): rate-limited by the rules there are.
5. **Others see it** in the snapshot and appeared records, and a change reaches those in view once (decide: a delta flag or the job change's way). Measure the bytes per player before and after.
6. **Drawing.** Outfit pieces join the one merged mesh per character (T-706's `DressPlan`): they replace the job's pieces and the worn gear of the slots they cover in the drawing only; `hides_hair` counts. Weapons stay visible unless you say why not. Still one draw call per character; measure the 40-player crowd as T-706 did, before and after.
7. **The wardrobe panel**, from the HUD: the owned outfits by slot, what shows, a turning preview (`LookView`) that shows exactly what others see, and a switch that hides every outfit. 48 dp, safe area, text scale to 1.5, words in `UiText`, in the `ui` matrix with 0 problems. Nothing redrawn while nothing changes, except the open preview.
8. **Erasing** (T-703): whatever is stored goes with the account; the erasure test must see it.
9. **Docs:** ARCHITECTURE, THEMES and FORKING (how a fork adds an outfit, and what never to do: stats), VOCABULARY.
10. **Tests** for every rule, with the real database for saving and erasing, hostile messages for the intent, and one e2e run: get an outfit, put it on, a second client sees it, log out, come back, it is still on, take it off.
