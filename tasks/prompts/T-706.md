### Goal
Players choose how their character looks on a creation screen with a rotating 3D preview, and everyone in the world sees it.

### Requirements
1. **Drawing it in the world.** Body, face, hairstyle, skin tone and hair colour are drawn from the ids T-705 sends, through the theme's asset keys and the §7.7 fallbacks. Anything missing falls back and warns once, never crashes.
2. **Jobs.** Characters are drawn today by their job's model (`model.char.warden` and so on). Decide how a job stays recognisable with appearance bodies, as data a fork can change, and say why. Monsters and NPCs don't change.
3. **§9 budgets.** Still one draw call per character: face and hair join the merge in `GearMesher`, skin and hair colours are baked into the vertex colours, and equal looks share the cache. A headgear that hides the hair is honoured (data in the manifest or item visual: document it; the placeholder theme may show at most one example). Weigh the number of combinations: say what bounds the merged-mesh cache, and what a crowd of 40 different looks costs (draw calls, triangles, memory, and frame time). Measure before and after, headless or hidden, with at most one windowed measuring run.
4. **The creation screen.** From character select: name, gender, face, hairstyle, skin tone and hair colour, with a 3D preview that rotates by itself and by drag, and a random button. It uses the same drawing code as the world, so the preview is what others see. The choices come from the bundle only; the server decides. Every refusal is shown. Back keeps what was chosen.
5. **Character select** shows each character's look.
6. **Phone.** 48 dp targets, landscape, safe area, text scale up to 1.5, words in `UiText`, the new screens in the `-Run ui` matrix with 0 problems. Colour swatches have a name for people who cannot tell the colours apart. No per-frame work while nothing changes (the rotating preview is the exception, only while it is open).
7. **Placeholder art** stays as it is. Real art is T-707 in a fork.
8. **Room for T-708:** say how outfits would join the same merge.
9. **Docs:** `docs/THEMES.md` and `docs/FORKING.md` gain what a fork must supply for appearance, where it changed.
10. **Tests.** The view model (defaults, gender switch keeping what fits, random with injected RNG, refusals); resolution and fallbacks; cache keys; hair hiding; `ThemeCheck` for anything new. One e2e run: register, create a character with non-default looks, enter, see it drawn with those ids, and a second client sees them too.
