### Goal
The owner asked for it: "auto attack nearby enemies, similar to our autopilot of quests, but auto attack nearby with auto skill also". A player turns on **auto-battle** and the server fights the monsters around them, using the skills on their skill bar, until they stop it, as quest autopilot does but with no quest.

### Requirements
1. **The same machinery, a second mode.** Build it on `AutopilotSystem` and `Game.Shared/Autopilot`, not beside them: one run per character, either quest autopilot or auto-battle, never both (starting one replaces the other). It only queues the intents a player would (rule 1, ARCHITECTURE §6.4.2 "It only decides"), uses `AutopilotSkill` for skills exactly as quest autopilot does (the skill bar in slot order, the same "has a use" rules, friendly skills when they would do something, no consumables), and ends the same ways (the player's own order, `stop`, death, leaving, the connection's end, the daily limit). Reuse, don't copy.
2. **Where it fights.** Around the cell where it was started (the **anchor**): it picks the nearest living monster by walking distance within `auto_battle.radius_cells` of the anchor (content), not in a safe zone, not one proven unreachable within `avoid_ms`, only what the player is sure to see. It never walks farther than the radius from the anchor, and goes back towards it when nothing is in reach, waiting there for respawns. A warp, a map change or a respawn ends the run (say why, or decide otherwise and say why). Retaliation as in quest hunting: whatever hit it within `retaliate_ms` is fought first, even a little outside the radius (bounded: say by how much).
3. **What it fights.** Decide the filter and put its numbers in content, for example: monsters within a level band of the player, never a boss-flagged one unless the player asked, and an option for "only those that attack me". The client may offer a choice the server validates; the server never fights what the filter refuses.
4. **Loot.** Decide whether it picks up what it killed (within the loot priority, weight and bag room, as quest autopilot's `gather_range` does for wanted items), and what happens when the bag is full (stop with its reason, as quest autopilot does). Say why.
5. **Staying alive.** No consumables (as quest autopilot). Friendly skills still heal when they have a use. Decide whether a run pauses or stops when HP falls below a share in content, and what the player sees.
6. **Limits.** It counts against the same daily limit as quest autopilot (`daily_limit_minutes`, shared, saved with the character), or a limit of its own in `balance.yaml`: decide and say why. Same per-tick cost caps. `auto_battle.enabled` false refuses it.
7. **Client**: an **Auto** toggle on the HUD (within the HUD budget on the smallest phone at the largest UI scale), its state in the existing autopilot indicator ("Stop auto-battle: fighting" / "returning" / "waiting"), the filter choice if any, refusals and ends on the flash line with their reasons. Only the server's word decides what the indicator shows. All strings in `UiText`; the new states in the `ui` matrix with 0 problems; say how you measured against ARCHITECTURE §9.
8. **Docs**: ARCHITECTURE §6.4 (a new 6.4.x "Auto-battle"), the client section, §10 messages, VOCABULARY (every new term), ADR-002 rows from 520.
9. **Tests** for every rule: picks the nearest in the radius, never leaves it, returns to the anchor and waits, retaliation, the filter, skill choice (shared with quest autopilot, so its tests keep passing), loot and full bag, low HP, the daily limit shared or not, replacing a quest run and being replaced, the player's order ending it, cost under a crowd of runs. One e2e run: a scripted client turns auto-battle on next to monsters, kills at least two with a skill used at least once, does not leave the radius, then a manual move ends it.
