### Goal
Players can found a guild, invite and accept members, give them ranks with permissions, write a notice, talk on a guild channel, and show an emblem chosen from the theme's set. Guilds are stored and hold on every server process that shares the database.

### Requirements
1. **Founding**: a character founds a guild with a name and an emblem. Name rules like character names (length, characters, reserved words: reuse what character naming uses, numbers in content), unique ignoring case; the cost to found (currency, an item, or nothing) and the member limit in content. One guild per character. Founding is one transaction with its cost (rule 10).
2. **Members and ranks**: invite (by name, or tapping a nearby player; answered like T-902's invite, with a block reading as "not online"), accept or decline, expiry, leave, kick. Ranks: a leader rank and a set of ranks the leader names and orders, each with permissions from a fixed vocabulary (invite, kick, edit notice, set ranks below one's own, and so on: a term each). Nobody acts on a member of the same or a higher rank. Hand over leadership. The last member leaving, or the leader leaving with nobody to take over by a rule you state, disbands the guild. Every change is a transaction that re-checks permissions in the database, so two officers acting at once on two server processes cannot break the rules.
3. **Notice**: one text, length in configuration, the same text rules as chat (T-901: format characters removed, over-long refused); members see it on sign-in and when it changes.
4. **Guild chat**: register a `guild` channel with `ChatService.Register`; mute, rate limit, blocks and reports work as on every channel. Members on another server process: say whether v1 reaches them (T-901's whispers are per process too) and how a follow-up would.
5. **Emblem**: chosen from a set the theme lists by asset key (rule 3), never uploaded; validated at load; the set and its keys in the theme's manifest and the client bundle, the placeholder theme ships a few made by the theme generator or plain shapes. Shown on the guild panel and above members' names where names show, within the HUD and draw budgets.
6. **Online list**: members see who is online (on this process at least: say what holds for others), rank and level; no member's map or cell unless you decide so and say why.
7. **Erasing** (T-703): an erased account's characters leave their guilds (a leader hands over by the same rule); a guild left empty is deleted with its name freed. A deleted character leaves too. Update STORE_COMPLIANCE.md (§2 tables, the export (T-806) holds the account's guild memberships, the age-rating note on guild chat) and the erasure test that reads every table.
8. **Client**: a guild panel (members with rank and online state, the notice, the emblem, actions by permission), founding with the emblem picker, the invite prompt, the guild channel in the chat switch; all strings in `UiText`; the new states in the `ui` matrix with 0 problems on the smallest phone at the largest UI scale; say how you measured against ARCHITECTURE §9.
9. **No client authority**: permissions are checked by the server only; the client only hides what a rank cannot do.
10. **Docs**: ARCHITECTURE (a guild section, §3 if saving is touched, §10 messages), VOCABULARY (every term and permission), STORE_COMPLIANCE.md, ADR-002 rows from 460.
11. **Tests** for every rule, with the real database where it matters: founding and its cost atomically, name uniqueness racing, invite flow, rank permissions table, same-or-higher rank refused, two officers racing on two stores, leader hand-over and disband, notice text rules, guild chat with mute, emblem validation, erasing and deletion. One e2e run: a character founds a guild, invites a second (it accepts), they talk on the guild channel, the leader edits the notice and promotes the second, the second leaves.
