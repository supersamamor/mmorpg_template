### Goal
The maps come alive: every `spawns` entry in a map grid (T-201) puts its monsters in the world, monsters wander, notice players,
chase them, give up and walk home — all driven by `MonsterDef.ai` and `balance.yaml`, never by code constants. When a monster dies
(the event comes from combat, T-302), it respawns after the spawn's respawn time. Monsters **decide** to attack but don't resolve hits:
that is T-302, which will own the attack intent this task emits.

### Requirements
1. **Spawner:** on instance start, create every spawn's `count` monsters on random walkable cells inside its area (seeded `IRandom`,
   deterministic). Each monster entity gets `Kind = Monster`, `DefinitionId` = the monster id (so clients see its model through the AOI),
   and a `StatSheet` from the stats pipeline with the monster's `stats` overrides (so it has `move_speed`, `max_hp`, …). It remembers its
   spawn entry and home cell. On `EntityDied` for one of its monsters (published by T-302; tests publish it directly), the spawner despawns it
   and respawns a fresh one after `respawn` seconds of **simulated** time, in the same area. Count never exceeds the spawn's `count`.
2. **AI state machine** (data-driven, one system, monsters processed in entity-id order):
   - `idle` → occasionally `wander` to a random walkable cell near home (timings and radius from `balance.yaml` `ai`).
   - **Aggressive** monsters (`ai.aggressive`) notice the nearest player within `ai.aggro_range` cells that they have **line of sight** to
     (T-203 `LineOfSight`) → `chase`. Passive monsters only react when attacked (T-302 will publish a damage event; subscribe to `DamageDealt`
     and handle it — tests publish it directly).
   - `chase`: re-path toward the target with `MoveTo` no more often than a data-driven interval; stop when within `attack_range`
     (Chebyshev) → `attack` state, which raises an `AttackRequested`-style intent/event for T-302 at the monster's attack rate (`aspd` from its
     stats) — document exactly what T-302 must consume.
   - Give up → `return` home when the target is gone (left map, dead, disconnected-and-despawned) or the monster is more than `ai.chase_range`
     cells from **home**; while returning it ignores players; at home → `idle`. Optionally regenerate to full while returning — if you do, make
     it a `balance.yaml` switch.
3. **No hardcoded behavior numbers:** wander interval range, wander radius, re-path interval, and any "think" interval live in a new
   `ai` block in `BalanceDef` / `balance.yaml` (with comments, validated > 0, schema regenerated). Per-monster values come from `MonsterDef.ai`.
4. **Cheap at scale:** monsters with no player within the AOI view distance are **dormant** — they don't scan, path, or wander (but their
   respawn timers still run). Finding players near a monster must not scan every entity: add a small read-only spatial query on the instance
   (or reuse the AOI buckets through a narrow interface) — systems still don't call each other's logic (hard rule 7). Explain the choice.
5. **Events** (add to `EventNames` + VOCABULARY.md as needed): spawned (reuse `EntitySpawned` if it fits), aggro gained / lost (for later
   sound and UI), and the attack request for T-302. Keep reason terms as vocabulary constants.
6. **Tests** (fake clock, small test grids and the sample `map.starter_meadow`): spawn count and area per spawn entry; determinism (same
   seed → same positions and AI decisions); wander stays near home; aggressive aggro needs range **and** LOS; passive aggro only after
   `DamageDealt`; chase re-paths and stops at attack range; attack request rate follows `aspd`; leash: beyond `chase_range` → return,
   ignoring players; target despawns → return; respawn after the right simulated time and only up to `count`; dormancy (no thinking without
   nearby players; wakes when one approaches).
7. **Cost:** log the AI + spawner tick cost on the sample meadow with its full spawn list and ~20 players walking around, and with all
   monsters dormant. Log, don't assert wall time; assert allocations per tick are bounded after warm-up.
