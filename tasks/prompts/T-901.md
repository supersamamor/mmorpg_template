### Goal
Players can talk: to those on the same map, and to one character by name. Chat is a generic system of **channels**, so party (T-902) and guild (T-904) add a channel by giving it its members, without changing chat.

### Requirements
1. **Channels.** `map` (everybody on the sender's map instance; decide whether it is the whole map or the area of interest, and say why), `whisper` (one online character by name; an answer when nobody of that name is online, which tells no more than that), and `system` (server lines: T-806's announcements may stay as they are; say which). A channel kind is a vocabulary term. Party and guild are not built: leave one seam, a channel whose members something else supplies, with a test double proving it, and no party or guild code.
2. **The server decides.** The client sends an intent (channel, target, text); the server checks it and sends the line with the sender's character name and id. Text is trimmed, at most a length you set in configuration or content (not a C# number; rule 2), and control and invisible characters are removed or refused (decide, and test right-to-left and zero-width tricks). A line is never echoed before the server has accepted it.
3. **Mute** (T-806): every line goes through `ModerationChecks.MaySpeakAsync` first; a muted sender gets its own answer with how long is left, and the line goes nowhere. No call per line to the database beyond that check's cache.
4. **Rate limit**: per account, so many lines per window and a burst (numbers in configuration, not in code); over it, the line is refused with its own answer. Say why per account and not per connection.
5. **Blocking**: a player can block another character; that character's lines no longer reach them (whispers are refused for the sender with the same answer as "not online", so a block does not show). Decide where blocks live (on the server, saved, or on the device) and why; a server-side list goes with the account when it is erased.
6. **Reporting**: a player can report a line they received. The server keeps what was said, who said it (ids), who reported it and when; the client never sends the text it claims was said (the server keeps recent lines for that long enough to be reported, in memory, and says how long). Staff see open reports and close them through the admin API (`support`+), audited. Reports and the lines in them follow docs/STORE_COMPLIANCE.md: say how long they are kept, and erase them with either account.
7. **Filter** (optional, decide): if you add a word filter, its list is content or theme data, never in C#, and a fork can leave it empty. Say what it does to a line (refuse or mask).
8. **Client**: a chat panel on the HUD for a phone in landscape: a short log that does not cover the play area, a channel switch, a whisper target, and the on-screen keyboard (Godot's virtual keyboard on Android and iOS; check what covers what when it is up). Names are never coloured or shaped by text the sender typed. All strings in `UiText`, the new states in the `ui` matrix with 0 problems, within ARCHITECTURE §9; say how you measured.
9. **Nothing logged that a player wrote**, except what a report keeps. No chat history on the device beyond what is on screen in this session.
10. **Docs**: ARCHITECTURE (a chat section, §10 messages, §12 reads), ADMIN_API.md, VOCABULARY, STORE_COMPLIANCE.md (chat now exists: §2 tables, §3 and §4 answers, age rating note), ADR-002 rows from 400.
11. **Tests** for every rule, with the real database where it matters: channel routing (map, other map, whisper, offline, blocked), the seam with a double, mute with the cache, rate limit at the edges, text rules, report content and the client never supplying the text, staff read and close with roles and audit, erasing with either account, nothing written by a player in the logs. One e2e run: two scripted clients on one map talk, whisper, one blocks the other, one reports a line, staff mutes the sender through the admin API and its next line is refused.
