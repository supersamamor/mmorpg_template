### Goal
Two players next to each other can trade items and currency with a two-step confirmation; the swap is atomic in the world and in the database, and no sequence of actions, disconnects, crashes or races can create or lose an item or a coin (rule 10).

### Requirements
1. **Asking**: by tapping a nearby player or from the party panel; the other accepts or declines; it expires (time in content or configuration). Refusals have their own terms (busy in another trade, too far, dead, not online, blocked: answered like "not online", as T-902 does). Rate-limit asks per account. Range and the trade's size limits (entries per side) in content.
2. **The window**: each side offers items (whole entries or part of a stack) and currency from what it carries; worn items, and anything content marks as not tradeable (add the flag to item definitions if there is none, default tradeable, validated at load), cannot be offered. Both see both offers as the server holds them.
3. **Two steps**: each side *locks* its offer; any change by either side unlocks both. When both are locked, each *confirms*; the swap happens only when both have confirmed the same two locked offers (check a version, not "something was confirmed"). Moving away out of range, dying, being attacked if you decide so, warping, a disconnect or a server stop cancel the trade with its own reason; nothing moves.
4. **The swap** runs on the tick in one step for both characters: re-check everything (both still carry every offered unit, weight and entry limits on the receiving side, currency limits), then move all or nothing. Items keep their permanent ids, cards and refine; a part of a stack becomes a new entry with a new id on the receiving side. Publish an event each side's systems can use (quests, logs).
5. **Saving**: both characters' saves after the swap must land together or not at all, so a crash between the two can never leave an item on both or on neither. Use T-702's save links if they do this (say how they do); otherwise the restricted step above. Test with the real database: a crash (a save that never lands) on either side, a save racing the swap, a server stop during the trade.
6. **Dupe safety**: a test per known trick: offering the same entry twice, dropping, selling, using, equipping or refining an offered item during the trade, changing the offer after the other side confirmed, trading from two clients of one account, a disconnect and resume in the middle, two trades at once with a third player. None creates or loses anything; property-style tests over random sequences of these, counting every item id and coin before and after.
7. **Logs**: a trade writes one structured log line with both character ids, what moved (item ids, entry ids, quantities, currency) and when, for support and dupe audits; never names or anything else about the person. Say how long logs are kept (docs/STORE_COMPLIANCE.md: the server keeps no log files; the fork decides).
8. **Client**: the trade window on a phone in landscape (both offers, a quantity picker for stacks, currency, lock and confirm, the other side's state, why it was cancelled), the ask prompt, within the HUD budgets and ARCHITECTURE §9; all strings in `UiText`; the new states in the `ui` matrix with 0 problems on the smallest phone at the largest UI scale; say how you measured. The confirm step must show the final two offers so a last-moment change cannot fool a player.
9. **No client authority**: the client never says what is in an offer beyond its intents, who is in range, or that a swap happened.
10. **Docs**: ARCHITECTURE (a trade section, §3 if saving changes, §10 messages), VOCABULARY, ADR-002 rows from 440.
11. **Tests** for every rule above; the e2e run: two scripted clients trade an item and currency, both see it after the swap, one restarts its server process (or the e2e's equivalent of a stop) and still has what it got; a second trade is cancelled by walking away and nothing moved.
