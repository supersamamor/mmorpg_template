### Goal
The combat loop closes on the server. Monsters' `AttackRequested` events (T-301) and players' attack intents become real hits through the
existing `CombatResolver` (T-106). HP drops, entities die, the spawner respawns monsters (it already listens to `EntityDied`), killers
get base and job EXP through `ProgressionRules` (T-105), and drop tables roll into ground items. All of it runs in the tick, is
deterministic with the instance's seeded `IRandom`, and is driven by content and `balance.yaml`.

This task is **server world only**. The wire side is a later task because it lives in the restricted `src/Game.Server/Net/**`:
- `attack` / `respawn` client messages;
- HP in view records;
- combat events sent to observers.

Tests drive everything through intents and events in-process.

### Requirements
1. **Vitals.** Every combat-capable entity (player, monster) has a current HP (and SP, unused for now) next to its `StatSheet`.
   - It starts at `max_hp` when the entity spawns.
   - It is clamped to the new `max_hp` when stats change.
   - It never goes below 0.

   Keep this in a combat-owned table keyed by `EntityId`, or as small fields on `Entity`; explain your choice. Players get their vitals
   without touching `Net/**`: pick them up from the instance's player list or from a world event.
2. **Attacks.**
   - **Monster attacks:** consume `AttackRequested`. Re-check on the tick that both entities are alive, on the same map, and within the
     request's range (Chebyshev). Otherwise drop it and count the reason (vocabulary terms).
   - **Player attacks:** a new world intent `AttackTarget(entity, target)` means "keep auto-attacking this monster until it dies, I stop,
     or it becomes unreachable". `StopAttack` ends it. A new `MoveTo` / `StopMove` from the player also ends it.
   - **Player approach and rate:** the player walks into range with `MoveTo` intents, like the AI does: no direct calls into movement,
     and a re-path limit from data. The player attacks at its `aspd` (attacks per second). Its range is a `balance.yaml` value until
     weapons exist (T-401).
   - **Rules:**
     - Players can only target monsters (no PvP in v1).
     - A player inside a `safe` zone can't start an attack and can't be hit. A monster's request against them is dropped, with a
       reason. Use the grid's zones.
   - **Resolution:**
     - Every hit goes through `CombatResolver` with one reused `AttackContext` per system.
     - Monster element, race and size come from `MonsterDef`. Weapon inputs are 0 until T-401.
     - No allocation per hit after warm-up.
   - **Events:** publish `DamageDealt` for every resolved attack (miss = 0, plus a hit/crit/miss flag) and `EntityDied` when HP reaches 0.
     Both are already consumed by T-301.
3. **Death.**
   - **Shared:** a dead entity gets `Dead = true` and stops moving (`StopMove`). It receives no more hits, and requests from it are dropped.
   - **Monster:** the spawner already despawns it on `EntityDied`. Check that the ordering works: death publishes in phase 4, the spawner
     reacts. Add a test.
   - **Player:**
     - Stays dead on its cell until a `Respawn(entity)` intent.
     - Then it is revived at the map's respawn point with a `balance.yaml` share of `max_hp`. The point is the `new_character` start cell
       for now; say so and propose a data field for later.
     - An optional base EXP penalty on death, as a `balance.yaml` percent of the current level's requirement. Never lose a level. 0 is
       allowed.
4. **EXP distribution.**
   - **Who earns:** when a monster dies, every player who damaged it and is still on the map earns EXP. Track damage per attacker since
     the monster's last full-HP moment.
   - **Split:** shares are proportional to damage dealt. Integer math that is deterministic and documented, with no EXP lost or created:
     the shares add up exactly to `MonsterDef.BaseExp` / `JobExp`. Pick and document a tie-break for the remainder.
   - **Applying it:**
     - EXP goes through `ProgressionRules.GainBaseExp` / `GainJobExp`.
     - Players have no stored progression yet, so keep an in-memory `ProgressionState` per player character. Create it with
       `ProgressionRules.NewCharacter(new_character.start_job_id)`. T-702 will persist it; leave a clear seam.
     - On level up, the player's `StatSheet` must reflect the new levels (recompute), and current HP is handled as in 1.
   - **Events:** publish an `ExpGained` event per player, and pass through or wrap the progression events (level up, points gained) for
     later UI and quests. Don't call quests (rule 7).
5. **Drops.**
   - **Rolling:** on a monster's death, roll each of its `DropTableIds` with the instance random. Every entry rolls independently:
     `chance`, then a quantity in `min..max`.
   - **Ground items:**
     - Each drop becomes a ground item entity (`EntityKind.Item`, `DefinitionId` = the item id, quantity), on a walkable cell at or near
       the death cell. Spread rule from data; never on a blocked cell.
     - It has **loot priority**: only the top damager may claim it for `balance.yaml` milliseconds, then anyone. Store the owner and
       times. Claiming itself is T-401, but expose the "may X pick this up now?" rule as a pure function and test it.
     - It despawns after a `balance.yaml` lifetime of simulated time.
   - **Events:** publish `ItemDropped`, and `ItemExpired` when it despawns.
   - **Data audit:** check the sample monsters' drop tables, and list in your reply any monster with no drops or a suspicious chance.
     Don't rebalance them.
6. **Data, not code.** New `balance.yaml` numbers, each with a comment, validated, and with the schema regenerated:
   - the player unarmed attack range;
   - the player chase re-path interval;
   - the respawn HP share;
   - the death EXP penalty percent;
   - the loot priority duration;
   - the ground item lifetime;
   - the drop spread radius.

   Group them in sensible blocks (e.g. extend `combat`, add `death` and `loot`). No monster or item ids and no tuning numbers in C#.
7. **Events and vocabulary.** Add every new event (`ExpGained`, `ItemDropped`, `ItemExpired`, death and respawn of players if separate,
   attack-dropped reasons) to `EventNames` and VOCABULARY.md. Document in the reply exactly what each carries, because quests (T-602), party
   (T-902) and the wire follow-up will consume them.
8. **Tests** (fake clock, seeded random, small test grids and the sample meadow):
   - **Hits:** a monster `AttackRequested` produces `DamageDealt` with the resolver's damage, and HP goes down by exactly that. A miss
     deals 0. Golden values: one scripted fight with a fixed seed gives the same damage sequence and outcome every run.
   - **Refused requests:** out of range, dead attacker, dead target, safe zone.
   - **Player attack:** it walks into range, attacks at `aspd`, and stops on death, `StopAttack` or a new `MoveTo`. It cannot target a
     player or start inside a safe zone.
   - **Monster death:** `EntityDied` once; the spawner respawns it after `respawn` (end to end with T-301's systems).
   - **Player death and respawn:** HP share, cell, EXP penalty, and never below the level floor.
   - **EXP split:** one attacker gets everything; two attackers 70/30; three with a remainder add up exactly; an attacker who left the
     map gets nothing. Level up through a kill updates the stats.
   - **Drops:** a 100% entry always drops, a 0% entry never does, and quantity stays within min..max. Determinism per seed. Items never
     land on blocked cells. Loot priority over time. Expiry.
9. **Cost.** Log the combat system's tick cost on the sample meadow with its full spawn list and ~20 players fighting monsters. Log
   wall time without asserting it. Assert allocations per tick are bounded after warm-up (use `AllocationProbe` in `tests/Game.Tests`).
